Cleaning and missing values: listings.csv
================================================================================
Rows: 91,954 -> 59,846
Columns: 90 -> 78

Steps taken
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Dropped 14 completely empty columns, because they hold no information: neighborhood_overview, host_since, host_response_time, host_response_rate, host_acceptance_rate, host_thumbnail_url, host_neighbourhood, host_total_listings_count, host_verifications, neighbourhood, neighbourhood_group_cleansed, calendar_updated, license, instant_bookable. bathrooms is kept, because it is rebuilt from bathrooms_text.
2. Removed 0 duplicate listings (same id).
3. Converted price from text such as '$1,180.00' to a number by removing the '$' and the thousands commas.
4. Dropped 32,049 rows without a price. Price is the main variable for analysis, so filling it in would invent the values we want to study. These listings are mostly not bookable: 87% have no available days in the next year (against 0.5% of the listings with a price), and 93% have source = 'previous scrape'.
5. Dropped 59 rows with a price above the 99.9th percentile of their own room type (Entire home/apt 6,798, Hotel room 1,440, Private room 5,000, Shared room 2,083; the highest price was 75,000). A price that is normal for an entire home can be an error for a single room. Prices this extreme distort averages and scaling, so they are removed before the 99th-percentile flag below.
6. Kept 2,580 listings with a price and source = 'previous scrape' (4.3% of the rows). Their median price is 149 against 176 for the others, so nothing shows their prices are wrong. They do have fewer available days (median 32 against 260).
7. Flagged 599 listings with a price above the 99th percentile (1,203.38) in price_above_p99. They are kept, because they may be genuine luxury listings.
8. Filled 59,754 missing bathrooms from the number in bathrooms_text (e.g. '1.5 baths' -> 1.5).
9. Filled 92 missing bathrooms with the median of listings that accommodate the same number of guests.
10. Filled 23 missing bedrooms with the median of listings that accommodate the same number of guests.
11. Filled 1,502 missing beds with the median of listings that accommodate the same number of guests.
12. Filled 12,226 missing reviews_per_month with 0, only for listings with no reviews.
13. Added a has_reviews column (12,226 listings have no reviews). Their review scores are left missing, because filling them in would give unreviewed listings a typical score they never got.
14. Filled 3 missing has_availability from availability_365: 't' when the listing has any available days in the next year.
15. Converted the 't'/'f' columns to True/False: host_is_superhost, host_has_profile_pic, host_identity_verified, has_availability. A missing value stays missing; it is not turned into False.
16. Stored counts such as bedrooms and beds as whole numbers (1, not 1.0).

What the action means
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
column dropped: every row was empty. filled: see the steps above for how. rows dropped: the gap was only in rows that were removed. kept missing: left on purpose, for these reasons:
- first_review, last_review and the review scores: a listing with no reviews has no date or score, and filling it in would give it one it never got.
- description, host_location, host_about: free text, not filled with made-up text.
- source: its only value is 'previous scrape'; it is a label.
- bathrooms_text, price_quote_* and the minimum/maximum nights columns: small gaps that the price analysis does not need.
